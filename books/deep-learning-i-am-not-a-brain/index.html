What if the machine told you, in its own words, exactly what it is?

Deep Learning is not a brain and does not pretend to be one. It is the same weighted sum from Book One, squashed through a curve, stacked thirty layers deep and corrected by the same downhill walk, this time run backwards through every layer at once. The book is narrated by the machine itself: it explains what it is made of, how it is corrected, how it learns to see, to remember and to answer, then says plainly what none of it amounts to. Nine parts, thirty chapters, one exact answer to a question somebody wrote down.

Thirty chapters in nine parts · 388 pages · 43 full-page infographics.
Nothing is claimed that the chapter's own verification script did not first prove; twenty-five of the thirty chapters changed a claim because it did.

Nine parts, and each is a question the machine answers about itself: what it is made of, how it is corrected, how it can be right about what it has not seen, how it learns to see, to remember, to look instead of remembering, to grow, to be asked, and finally, to make. The same three ideas from Book One, stacked until they see, remember, and answer.
