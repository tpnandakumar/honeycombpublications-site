What if a machine could tell you a story?

Machine learning is not magic and it is not a black box. It is arithmetic: a straight line fitted through a scatter of points, a slope followed downhill, one probability multiplied by another. This book opens with a line drawn through a handful of house prices and closes with eigenvectors, maximum likelihood, and a proof that the two cost functions the whole field runs on are one principle under two assumptions.

Twenty-one chapters · 384 pages · 59 full-page infographics.
Nothing is asserted that has not been derived, and every formula is built from the one before it.

Five moves, and every chapter is one of them: take the raw facts, find what actually matters in them, build the understanding a step at a time, make the prediction, then learn from the mistake and go again. Lines and least squares. Gradients and descent. Probability, Bayes and the arithmetic of doubt. Trees, forests, eigenvectors and the SVD. Then the Python to run all of it.
